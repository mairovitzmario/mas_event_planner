# Total Event Planning Negotiation Summary

## Loop 1 Negotiation Drama
The negotiation process was characterized by intense interpersonal conflicts and a major intervention from the Treasurer. Guests initially demanded highly specific, conflicting seating arrangements—particularly regarding Alice and Charlie’s mutual antipathy and Eve’s refusal to sit near carnivores like Bob. As the Host attempted to resolve these social puzzles, the Treasurer intervened to shut down the logistical complexity, mandating a consolidated menu and a single rectangular table to stay under a strict $500 budget.

In the final iteration, the Host successfully satisfied the Treasurer by merging the menus into two tiers and placing everyone at one table. While most social buffers were maintained to keep the peace, total consensus was impossible because some guests, like Charlie, had their specific social preferences overridden to satisfy the Host's priority of enforcing Alice's negative constraints.

## Loop 2 Negotiation Drama
The negotiation process was defined by a volatile clash between high-maintenance social requirements and the Treasurer’s rigid fiscal mandates. Guests initially bombarded the Host with contradictory seating and dietary requests, resulting in a failed circular seating draft that was rejected by almost every participant due to proximity issues or the presence of the 'buffer seat.'

Upon the Treasurer's intervention, the Host pivoted to a linear seating arrangement and a consolidated menu to slash costs. While this successfully satisfied the financial constraints and resolved the physical proximity conflict between the vegan and carnivore factions, it left several guests unhappy. Specifically, Alice, Charlie, and Bob were forced to sacrifice their specific proximity requirements to comply with the new logistics, leaving their individual demands unfulfilled.

## Loop 3 Negotiation Drama
The negotiation process was marked by intense friction between the guests' highly specialized, non-negotiable dietary and seating requirements and the Treasurer’s strict budgetary and operational constraints. Guests like Bob and Eve expressed extreme intolerance regarding the proximity of opposing dietary lifestyles, while others, specifically Alice and Charlie, had conflicting seating demands that made a simple arrangement impossible. The Treasurer further complicated matters by mandating a dramatic reduction in menu complexity and the elimination of physical 'buffers' between seats.

To bridge these divides, the host proposed a consolidated seating chart and a simplified dual-menu structure. While this successfully resolved the budget issues and satisfied some proximity requests, it failed to appease the most rigid demands—specifically Bob’s refusal to be in the same room as vegans and the guests' insistence on medical-grade kitchen safety. Consequently, despite the host's efforts to create a unified plan, several guests remain highly dissatisfied with the compromise regarding their safety and social comfort.
